Seven Names Listed, One Clause Missing

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 24, 2026 3 min read

On November 23, 2026, the Bureau of Industry and Security added seven companies to the Entity List, effective the same day. The lead name is Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd. of Singapore. The other six are Apex Enterprise Solutions and Swiftdata Pte. Ltd. in Singapore, PT Megaspeed International, MSFusion Sdn. Bhd. and Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd in Malaysia, and Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. in Thailand. The final rule goes to the Federal Register on November 25. Every export of an item subject to the Export Administration Regulations to any of the seven now needs a licence, and BIS will review those licences under a presumption of denial.

What BIS actually said

The stated basis is narrow and specific: a significant risk that these firms divert advanced computing items to unauthorized end users or end uses. Cargo already in transit can still be delivered under the prior treatment if the transfer completes within 30 days of publication. The timeline behind the names is longer than the headline suggests. Commerce opened its investigation in 2024. It sought Nvidia’s sales records for Megaspeed in May 2025, and Nvidia put the firm on its do-not-sell list in June 2025. A joint Commerce and Justice Department statement on November 16, 2026 confirmed that Justice now leads the criminal inquiry. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on November 13 that other agencies had favoured this designation for months, adding: “but the pen is in Commerce’s hand.”

One detail tells you how long these firms have been watched. The Singapore parent is listed under four aliases, including 7Road International, its name before a rebrand, with a registered address at the Ocean Financial Centre. The Thai entity carries the same alias.

The clause that is missing

Chris McGuire, who ran technology and national security at the National Security Council, read the rule and said the quiet part on X: BIS omitted the foreign direct product rule, the provision that extends US jurisdiction to chips made abroad with American technology. Nvidia’s chips are manufactured in Taiwan. Without that provision, McGuire wrote, the listing has “zero effect” on Megaspeed buying AI chips, and he noted that every other Chinese entity listed over AI chip concerns was covered by that rule. He conceded one counterpoint: the BIS rule of November 19, 2026 already requires licences for advanced chip exports to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, so part of the gate was already shut.

My read

My position: price this as a scrutiny event, not as a new block on Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) shipments. The November 23 rule names no restriction on Nvidia itself, and the company had already cut Megaspeed off in June 2025. The investable question is whether the list stops at seven names. McGuire called Megaspeed the tip of the iceberg and named Novagate Cloud, a firm earlier reporting tied to the same personnel, as an obvious next candidate. If Commerce adds names like that one, regional cloud operators and their chip suppliers face a wider compliance bill. If the list stays at seven, Monday was a closing chapter on a probe that began in 2024.

Watch three dates. First, November 25, when the published Federal Register text will show whether the foreign direct product provision appears in the final version. Second, December 1, the deadline House Foreign Affairs Democrats set on November 19 for Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to answer whether Nvidia interfered during the investigation. Third, December 3, when Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear alone at a public Senate Banking hearing on export control oversight, with Lutnick before the same committee on December 10. Those hearings, not the listing itself, will tell you whether this file widens.
