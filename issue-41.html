12,000,000 TPP Is the New Export Border

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 18, 2026 3 min read

On Monday, November 16, 2026, the Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule titled Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors. The public inspection copy is 117 pages, document 2026-23529, RIN 0694-AK50. BIS said the rule was written at the direction of the Treasury Department and the National Security Council, to stop circumvention through remote computing in third countries. The rule text is summarised here.

What the rule actually does

A new control, section 744.24 of the Export Administration Regulations, requires a licence to export, reexport or transfer advanced items, including ECCN 3A090 and 4A090 chips, where the exporter knows the item is headed for a data centre, anywhere in the world, that provides more than 12,000,000 aggregate total processing performance, or TPP, to companies headquartered in Macau or in a Country Group D:5 destination, which includes China. Linked data centres count toward the same total. A second provision requires a licence for advanced chip shipments to or within Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, which BIS calls higher than normal diversion risks. Applications are reviewed case by case. Shipments to a consignee headquartered in the United States are excluded, provided the exporter files monthly reports under section 743.10. The same rule formally rescinds the AI Diffusion Rule of January 2025, which BIS says was never enforced.

On the same Monday, Commerce and Justice published a timeline of their Megaspeed investigation: it began in 2024, Nvidia placed the Singapore cloud firm on its no sell list in June 2025 after Commerce requested records, and United States officials and Singapore police inspected Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang that same month. No conclusion on Megaspeed has been announced.

Two tiers, priced on Tuesday

The responses on November 17 split cleanly. Singapore, through the Ministry of Digital Development and Information and the Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry, said it was studying the details carefully and exploring how to facilitate trusted access for Singapore companies. Malaysia was blunter: Trade Minister Johari Abdul Ghani said Kuala Lumpur has no plans to negotiate and expects data centre operators to comply. China’s Foreign Ministry called the rule economic coercion on the same day.

Singapore’s market priced the split on November 17. DigiCore REIT fell 2.2 percent, Keppel DC REIT fell nearly 1 percent and NTT DC REIT slipped 0.5 percent. ST Engineering closed down 1.9 percent at $8.86. Singtel rose 2.5 percent to $4.10 after saying its RE:AI platform serves domestic public sector and research clients only, adding: “We are still assessing the implications, but expect the impact to be minimal.”

My read: the exemption is the business model now. A United States headquartered hyperscaler can keep shipping its own servers into a leased hall in Singapore, with monthly reports. An independent operator serving Chinese enterprise clients must now prove who owns its customers, all the way to the ultimate parent, or wait in a licence queue in Washington. That diligence burden, and BIS treating a data centre’s refusal to share customer ownership as a red flag, is where the cost lands.

Watch November 19. The rule takes effect on Federal Register publication, scheduled for that date, and publication starts both clocks: comments are due 60 days after it, and data centres already providing the covered volume to D:5 or Macau headquartered companies have 90 days from the effective date to obtain a licence. If Singapore wins trusted access language in that window, the list softens in practice. If it does not, regional landlords will have to name their exposure tenant by tenant on the next earnings calls.
