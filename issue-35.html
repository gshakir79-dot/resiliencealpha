Issue #35 Free Article Silicon Sovereignty

TSMC Put $1 Billion Into Its Own Customers

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 12, 2026 3 min read

TSMC filed its October numbers with the SEC on November 10: NT$586.89 billion in revenue, up 14.7 percent from September and 59.7 percent from October 2025, for a January to October total of NT$4,485.62 billion. One day later, its board approved up to $500 million in Anthropic common shares at the IPO price and up to $500 million in OpenAI preferred stock. The revenue filing is here.

What happened

The board meeting that produced the two stakes also approved about $40.93 billion in capital appropriations for advanced process capacity, advanced packaging, specialty technology, fab construction and 2027 research and development. It approved a further $500 million to set up an investment company for strategic bets in the semiconductor ecosystem. It also signed off on third quarter revenue of $45.50 billion and net income of $23.23 billion. Anthropic, for its part, filed on November 10 to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78, a raise of up to $64.35 billion, with a separate $26 billion private placement to investors including Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC, according to Seoul Economic Daily on November 11.

The one number

One billion dollars is 2.4 percent of the $40.93 billion the same board approved for capacity. Diversification is the wrong word for a sum that small. I read the two stakes as allocation insurance. Anthropic and OpenAI are both pursuing their own application specific chips for inference, according to the Taiwanese outlets cited by Seoul Economic Daily. Chips designed in California still need a foundry, and at the leading edge that foundry is TSMC. A $500 million cheque buys an early seat in the room where future wafer demand is planned, long before a purchase order shows up in a monthly revenue print.

The texture underneath

On November 11 in Taipei, Cliff Hou, TSMC senior vice president and deputy co-chief operating officer, told the Chinese National Federation of Industries 80th Industry Day that Taiwanese makers now account for 20 to 25 percent of back end advanced packaging equipment, against about 10 percent of front end process equipment, Economic Daily News reported. Hou flagged a gap in software and system integration. Put that next to the board vote: $40.93 billion for the physical layer, $1 billion for the customer layer, in the same week. Seoul Economic Daily also noted that Taiwanese coverage flagged Nvidia's high share of TSMC revenue as a risk the Anthropic and OpenAI stakes help to balance. I agree with that reading, with one correction: a stake does not balance revenue. It only buys information and goodwill ahead of the orders that might.

My position, and what to watch

My position: October proves demand today, and the board vote is about demand in 2028. If Anthropic and OpenAI custom silicon programmes grow, TSMC wants a seat in the room where those plans are made. Watch three dates. First, Anthropic's IPO pricing against the $70 to $78 range in its November 10 filing. Second, TSMC November revenue, due December 10 on its financial calendar: analysts cited by Seoul Economic Daily expect NT$538.1 billion to NT$569.9 billion a month for November and December if fourth quarter guidance of $52.3 billion to $54.3 billion is to hold, so a print above that band would say October was a level shift, not timing. Third, the January 14, 2027 investor conference, where audited fourth quarter results will show whether the $40.93 billion starts to bite into margins. Until those prints land, I am treating the $1 billion as cheap optionality, bought from strength.

The company covered in this issue:

TSM · Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company

NYSE: TSM and TWSE: 2330 · The world largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, headquartered in Hsinchu, Taiwan · Reported October 2026 revenue of NT$586.89 billion, up 59.7 percent year on year
