Two-Thirds Delivered, Three Days Left on the Paper

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 7, 2026 4 min read

On Friday morning, November 6, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer spoke by video with Vice Premier He Lifeng. The Treasury readout says Bessent told He that China must fully and immediately implement the commitments it made in Busan, and that continued progress depends on concrete results. Greer, in a separate USTR readout, named the test: concrete actions on agricultural purchases, rare earths, and other critical minerals. Bessent put the same line on X the same day. US officials told the Financial Times in September that Beijing was fulfilling only about two thirds of its rare earth pledge, and one person familiar with the talks said supply had not risen since Bessent raised the issue with He in New York in September.

The consensus says January. The paper says Tuesday.

Washington says the wider truce, extended when President Xi Jinping visited Washington in September, now runs to January 10, 2027. I am not pricing that date. I am pricing the date in law. MOFCOM and Customs Announcement No. 70 of 2025, the instrument that suspended the October 9, 2025 export control package, ends that suspension on November 10, 2026. That is Tuesday. No amended Announcement No. 70 matching the January date has been published. Customs officers and licensing clerks work from numbered notices. They do not work from interview lines about a truce. Until Beijing publishes a new notice, the legal cliff is three days away, and the October package includes the rule that reaches goods made outside China when Chinese rare earth content passes 0.1 percent of value.

The price gap is the delivery report

Total tonnage looks calm, and that calm misleads. Customs data reported by SMM put September rare earth shipments at 4,269.8 metric tonnes, up 6.7 percent from a year earlier and down 9.8 percent from August. January to September exports were 43,711 tonnes, down 9.6 percent year on year. The squeeze sits in the heavy grades. SMM quoted European dysprosium oxide on November 5 at $3,300 to $4,200 a kilogram, against $215 to $220 a kilogram inside China. European terbium oxide was $7,300 to $8,300 a kilogram. SMM said European inventories were tight and that dysprosium, terbium and gadolinium often had prices but no goods. That phrase belongs in every portfolio note on this trade. A quoted price with no goods behind it is a shortage with a number attached.

The licence that never paused

Even a clean extension on Tuesday would leave the harder regime in place. The April 2025 licensing system for seven medium and heavy rare earths was never suspended. Samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium still need a MOFCOM licence for each shipment. The October suspension covered an expansion. It never covered the base system. China accounted for 60 percent of mined magnet rare earths, 91 percent of refined output and 94 percent of sintered permanent magnet production in 2024, according to the IEA figures cited in the November 6 coverage. A two thirds delivery rate against that concentration gives Beijing room to comply in public and ration in private, shipment by shipment.

Washington is buying a buffer, not a fix

The United States answered with stockpile money, on the record and a day earlier. On November 5 the Defense Logistics Agency said it had committed $2 billion in fiscal year 2026 to acquire strategic and critical materials for the National Defense Stockpile. That is a real commitment, and it helps defence grades first. It does not create separation capacity by Tuesday, and it does not move a European terbium quote at $7,300 a kilogram. Builders outside China, including Energy Fuels (UUUU) with heavy rare earth capacity under construction at White Mesa in Utah, gain time and a clearer customer in Washington. Time is the asset here. Nobody in this chain has enough of it before November 10.

What I am pricing

My position: treat January 10 as a diplomatic claim and November 10 as the operative date until a numbered MOFCOM notice says otherwise. If that notice appears before Tuesday, the two thirds argument moves to APEC in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19, where President Trump and President Xi are expected, and to licence approval rates in the weeks after. If no notice appears, the October controls return in law on Tuesday, including the 0.1 percent extraterritorial rule, and every buyer who planned on January learns the difference between a truce and a text. Watch for the notice itself, by number, before the deadline. Nothing else settles it.
