Japan Is Paying for America’s Foundry in Malta

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 14, 2026 3 min read

GlobalFoundries broke ground on November 13, 2026 on a new 300mm fab at its Malta, New York site, the first facility under a United States and Japan initiative that GF describes as first of its kind. Japan will provide the investment. GF will provide the technology and run the plant. The company release is here, and the New York governor’s release is here.

What happened

The groundbreaking follows GF’s October 14, 2026 announcement that it was selected as technology provider and operator for two new fabs at Malta under an initiative providing up to $21 billion in funding. The first fab carries an initial investment of about $10 billion, supported by $570 million in New York State incentives, and is expected to come online in 2029. A second fab, at about $10 billion more, follows as the project advances. New York breaks its $570 million into $535 million in performance based Green CHIPS tax credits and $35 million in grants for workforce and infrastructure. The new building, Fab 8.2, will hold about 386,000 square feet of cleanroom. GF says the two fab initiative should create about 27,000 construction and ecosystem jobs and more than 1,000 manufacturing jobs.

Why it matters

Read the end markets in the release: automotive, industrial and connected infrastructure. This money buys foundational chips, the controllers and power and networking parts that stop car plants and factories when they run short. It does not buy leading edge AI capacity, and GF does not pretend otherwise. Tokyo is paying for trusted supply inside the United States, Washington gets a fab expansion without carrying the full capital bill, and GF gets growth funded by a sovereign partner. That is the trade deal working as industrial policy, with the invoices to prove it.

The one number

900,000. That is the number of 300mm wafers a year the first fab is expected to add when fully operational, with the second fab adding about the same again. Set that against GF’s current business. In the third quarter of 2026, reported November 4, GF posted revenue of $1.899 billion, up 13 percent year over year, with automotive revenue up 20 percent to $368 million and communications infrastructure and data center revenue up 81 percent to $316 million. The demand this fab serves is already the growing part of the book. The capacity to serve more of it arrives in 2029.

The texture underneath

Chief executive Tim Breen stood in Malta and said it plainly: “Today, we’re putting shovels in the ground and turning an ambitious partnership into reality.” Shovels in November 2026, wafers in 2029. Between those two dates sit construction risk, equipment lead times and a second fab decision that has funding language around it, not a start date.

My position, and what to watch

My position: this is the most honest kind of chip deal, because everyone can see what each side bought. Japan bought security of supply, the United States bought capacity onshore, and GF bought expansion without funding $21 billion alone. What it did not buy is speed. Watch three markers. First, whether the second fab gets a construction start date, not another funding headline. Second, GF’s fourth quarter results, guided to about $2.0 billion in revenue, for proof the automotive and data center growth holds. Third, the 2029 online date: any slip in the first year of construction tells you how real the rest of the timetable is.

GFS · GlobalFoundries Inc.
