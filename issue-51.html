Issue #51 Free Article Energy Leverage

The Ruler Says 4.9 Million. Baghdad Says 5.2.

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 28, 2026 3 min read

On Friday, November 27, 2026, three sources with knowledge of the talks told Reuters that OPEC+ has an agreement in principle to leave its output targets unchanged for the first quarter of 2027. Ministers hold four meetings on Sunday, November 29, starting at 1300 GMT. The freeze is the part everyone expected. The argument it postpones is the part that matters: how much oil each member can actually pump, measured this year for the first time by an outside firm.

What happened

The group commissioned its first ever review of members’ production capacity from DeGolyer and MacNaughton, a consultancy based in Dallas, Texas, and planned to use the results to set 2027 quotas. Delegates held a closing meeting on the review on November 16. Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said on Wednesday that ministers would assess progress on Sunday and that the group would complete the review by the end of the year. Completing it and agreeing with it are different things. Disputes over some of the estimates have delayed the process, so the collective target, reported at 31.01 million barrels a day, rolls over into 2027 instead of being rebuilt.

The war gives ministers their cover. On Thursday, US Central Command said an American aircraft disabled the Palau flagged bulk carrier Pacific Archer after it left Bandar Abbas and tried to run the blockade of Iranian ports. Central Command puts the tally at 158 commercial vessels redirected and six disabled since the blockade began in April. With Gulf exports still disrupted, Rapidan Energy Group expects no change to targets on Sunday, citing uncertainty over how long the Iran war lasts and what it does to output. A cartel that cannot agree on what its members can pump, and cannot predict what they can ship, does the rational thing. It freezes the number it already has.

The one number: 300,000 barrels a day

Preliminary findings put Iraq’s capacity at 4.9 million barrels a day, according to sources who spoke to Reuters earlier this week. Baghdad insists the figure is 5.2 million. The gap between the two numbers is 300,000 barrels a day. At Brent near $95 a barrel, where it ended the week, that gap is worth about $28.5 million a day in gross production value, roughly $10.4 billion a year. It also decides Iraq’s quota baseline for 2027 and beyond, which is why a technical audit has become a sovereignty argument. Quotas built on a contested measurement invite the oldest behaviour in this cartel: pump to the number you claim, not the number you were assigned. Angola walked out of OPEC in 2023 after a baseline fight of exactly this kind.

My read

My position: Sunday’s freeze says nothing about discipline. It says the sequence broke. The capacity review was supposed to end quota arguments by replacing politics with measurement. Instead the measurement became the argument, and the freeze buys the measurers time. Until the baselines are settled, every OPEC+ headline about unity is provisional, because the members have not yet agreed on what each of them is.

Watch the Sunday communique for two things: confirmation of the first quarter rollover, and any date for adopting the capacity baselines. Novak promised the review finished by December 31. If the baselines slip into 2027 alongside it, price the freeze as a truce between members, not a policy. And watch Baghdad first. A member told it can pump 300,000 barrels a day less than it believes is where the next quota breach shows up.

XOM · Exxon Mobil Corporation
