113 Names, Nearly 50 of Them Banks

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 16, 2026 3 min read

The Council of the EU listed 12 individuals and 101 entities on Monday, November 16, 2026. Nearly 50 of the entities are Russian banks and financial institutions. The Council published the release at 12:35 Brussels time, and the legal acts are already in the Official Journal as Council Decision (CFSP) 2026/2575 and Council Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2574. The release is here.

What Brussels actually listed

Read past the banks and the list widens in a deliberate order. It names the sole shareholder of Wildberries, the Russian retail company, identified by Barrot as its chief executive Tatyana Kim, and an individual controlling the majority stake in Ozon, another large Russian e-commerce company. It names energy companies that manage Russian refineries and the St. Petersburg oil terminal and work in exploration and production. For the shadow fleet, it names companies from the United Arab Emirates, China, Seychelles and Oman that buy crude oil products of Russian origin, and businesspersons who own or manage vessels already subject to EU measures and transport Russian oil in high-risk shipping. Everyone listed faces an asset freeze. Providing them with funds or economic resources, directly or indirectly, is prohibited. The individuals face an EU travel ban.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot told reporters in Brussels that the EU had approved 145 new designations on Monday, according to Reuters. The Council release for this framework counts 113. I work from the Council number, because the Official Journal names are the ones a compliance officer has to screen on Tuesday morning. The same ministers rejected an early discussion of Europe's security architecture with Moscow. Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys put it plainly to reporters: "It provides a lot of chances for Russia to get something out of it. So we shouldn't fall into this trap."

The one number

Nearly 50 banks. That is the number in this package, and it makes Monday a finance story before it makes Monday an oil story. Brent told you the same thing in real time. Reuters reported on November 16 that Brent futures fell 69 cents, or 0.7 percent, to $95.15 a barrel by 0919 GMT, as Hormuz shipments recovered and traders priced a fourth quarter surplus. A listing that moved crude would have shown up there. This one shows up in payment screening, correspondent banking, marine insurance and counterparty checks, where an EU touch on a transaction is now a legal problem with a name attached.

My position, and what to watch

My position: this package bites in compliance departments and payment rails first, and in physical oil cargoes only if the shadow fleet listings get enforced port by port. Watch three dated markers. First, Irish Foreign Minister Helen McEntee, whose country holds the EU presidency, said a further sanctions package is being finalised and will be discussed later this week. Second, Barrot said Paris will host a conference with Britain on December 4 on Russia's shadow fleet. Third, the listed buyers in the United Arab Emirates, China, Seychelles and Oman: if their cargoes keep loading under new names, Monday was paperwork. If loadings stall or insurance pulls back, Monday changed a trade. The Reuters report from Brussels is here.
