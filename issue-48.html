A $488 Million Valuation With $28.6 Million Behind It

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 25, 2026 4 min read

On November 24, 2026, Crown PropTech Acquisitions (OTC: CPTKW) filed a Form 8-K with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Attached was the investor presentation for its proposed business combination with Mkango Rare Earths Limited. The headline number is an implied pro forma enterprise value of $487.6 million for a company holding two projects: the Songwe Hill rare earth mine in Malawi and the Pulawy separation plant in Poland. Crown PropTech shareholders vote on the deal today, November 25, at 10:00 a.m. Eastern Time.

The pitch

The pitch writes itself, and management wrote it. Songwe Hill carries a net present value of $339.0 million on the presentation numbers, with initial capital costs of $325.5 million, operating costs of $22.3 per kilogram of total rare earth oxides in the first five years, an 18 year life and output of 5,954 tonnes of contained TREO a year in that period. Pulawy carries an NPV of $702.0 million, capital costs of $128.2 million, a 29 year life and capacity to take 30,000 tonnes a year of mixed rare earth carbonate feed at about 45 percent TREO, separating all of the neodymium and praseodymium in it. Add the two NPVs and you get $1.04 billion. Set the $487.6 million enterprise value next to that sum and the presentation calls the gap a 53 percent discount. Reserves total 18.1 million tonnes at 1.16 percent TREO.

The other page of the same presentation

Turn to the sources and uses table. Total sources are $28.6 million. Of that, $23.0 million is targeted PIPE proceeds. The word targeted is doing real work here: the 8-K says the parties are contemplating a private capital raise, and the presentation treats the PIPE figure as an assumption. The SPAC trust contributes an expected $4.8 million. Notes add $0.8 million. Total uses come to the same $28.6 million, split between $19.5 million of cash to the balance sheet and $9.1 million of transaction expenses.

So the cash this transaction actually delivers is $19.5 million, before a single tonne is mined. The two projects need $453.7 million of initial capital on the presentation figures. Development financing is a target for 2027. Construction is slated to start in 2027 at both sites, with full production at Songwe Hill in 2029 and at Pulawy in 2030. The one government commitment named in the presentation is $4.6 million from the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation.

One line in the filing deserves a second look. The illustration assumes about 83,822 public shares are redeemed for roughly $1.0 million at an estimated $12.05 a share, and it notes non-redemption agreements with BlackRock managed funds covering 400,000 public shares. At closing, public shareholders of the SPAC would own 0.8 percent of the combined company. Mkango Magnetic Materials, the parent, would own 78.8 percent. Read the filing in full and this is a Nasdaq listing with a small cash sidecar. The presentation is honest about that, provided you read past the discount slide.

My read

My position: price the 53 percent discount as arithmetic until the financing exists. A discount to NPV means something when the capital required to reach that NPV is funded. Here the funded part is $19.5 million of balance sheet cash against $453.7 million of build cost, and the bridge between the two is a financing round scheduled for next year. What a buyer of Mkango (TSX-V: MKA) is really pricing today is the probability that project finance, offtake money or a strategic partner arrives in 2027 on terms that leave the NPV intact. That may happen. Rare earth separation capacity outside China is scarce, and Pulawy would sit inside the European Union. Scarcity gives financiers a reason to come. It is not evidence that they have signed.

Watch the vote first, today at 10:00 a.m. Eastern, and read the closing release for the minimum cash condition and the final redemption count. Then watch for the one development that changes this story: a signed PIPE commitment or a named lender. Until one of those appears, the $1.04 billion is a pair of studies, and $487.6 million is the price tag attached to them.
