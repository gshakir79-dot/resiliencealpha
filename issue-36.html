The Seabed Got an Endorsement, Not a Mine

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 13, 2026 4 min read

The United States and Japan released a joint statement on seabed mineral development on November 12, 2026, after the second meeting of their bilateral working group on November 11 and 12 in Toyama. The statement covered Japan’s rare earth mud project near Minamitorishima Island and its polymetallic nodules project. It named no dollar figure, no permit, no company and no buyer. The State Department text is here.

The consensus take

The easy read is that two governments just moved seabed mining closer to commercial reality. The statement calls economic security cooperation an indispensable part of the alliance, promises clear and predictable rules to facilitate private investment, and says both governments will support commercial opportunities. Read quickly, that sounds like a green light. Read the whole document and the verbs stay in the future tense: plan to advance, deepen engagement, explore opportunities, facilitate exchanges, seek additional partnerships. Washington and Tokyo endorsed a direction. They committed to meetings, information sharing and regulatory talks.

What Japan has actually done

Japan has done real work, and the numbers deserve respect. In January and February 2026, the drilling vessel Chikyu ran a system connection test near Minamitorishima, about 1,900 kilometres southeast of Tokyo. The Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology reported on July 24 that about 50 tonnes of rare earth bearing mud were recovered, weighed after excluding water added during collection, from a seabed depth of about 6,000 metres. Analysis found roughly 54 percent of the rare earth content was medium and heavy elements, including yttrium, gadolinium and dysprosium. A detailed account of the trial is here. Fifty tonnes of mud is a proof of lifting. It is a long way from a production rate, and the agency has published no commercial cost per kilogram, no annual output figure and no project return.

The one number

February 2027. That is the date of the next step, a month long mining trial at the same site, with an integrated assessment including economics due by March 2028. So the earliest honest decision point on commercial production sits more than a year away, and any output would come after that decision. The joint statement changes none of those dates. It also gives away the real bottleneck in its own priority list: secure processing capacity among trusted partners. Mud lifted from 6,000 metres still needs dewatering, separation and refining. The statement lists that capacity as something to secure in future, which means it has not been secured yet.

The texture underneath

On November 20, the Foreign Press Center Japan will host Shoichi Ishii, program director for the Strategic Innovation Promotion Program work on the maritime security platform, to brief on the frontlines of deep sea rare earth mud research and the issues for practical implementation. That briefing title tells you where this project lives in November 2026: research and development, with practical implementation still framed as an open question by the people running it. I find that candour more useful than the alliance language around it.

My position, and what to watch

My position: treat the November 12 statement as an option, priced as an option, and refuse to price it as supply. It lowers political risk for seabed projects over time, and that matters for listed names with nodule claims and no revenue from them yet. It does nothing for dysprosium or terbium availability in 2027. Watch three markers. First, the February 2027 trial: tonnes per day, uptime and cost disclosure, not another photograph of mud in a bottle. Second, any named processing partner or offtake in the next working group readout, because that is the gap the November statement admitted. Third, the March 2028 assessment, where Japan has promised an economics verdict. Until the first of those lands, the seabed is a story about 2028 and later, told in 2026 headlines.
