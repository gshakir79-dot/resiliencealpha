Issue #43 Free Article Critical Minerals

105 Million Tonnes, Zero Licences to Dig

Marcus Vail Resilience Alpha Research November 20, 2026 3 min read

On Thursday, November 19, 2026, Kenya’s mining ministry issued a Notice of Intention to Award the Mrima Hill rare earths and niobium project in Kwale County to the Frontier Minerals Consortium. Mining Cabinet Secretary Hassan Joho named the group preferred bidder after months of competition. The consortium is Frontier Minerals LLC, Bunker Hill Mining Corp (TSX-V: BNKR), American Resources Corporation (NASDAQ: AREC) and ReElement Technologies. The ministry statement is here. Read the noun in that notice. Intention.

What Frontier actually won

Preferred bidder status, and a place at the front of a queue. Joho’s statement said each successful bidder must now “go to the ground, engage openly and respectfully with the host communities, landowners, county leadership, and relevant local stakeholders, and obtain all requisite consents as mandated by law.” His next sentence was blunter: “No prospecting operation can proceed without the social licence and formal consents of the people on the ground.” After consent comes a prospecting licence application through the Online Mining Cadastre, Mineral Rights Board review, an environmental and social impact assessment, and a feasibility study. Commercial mining needs a separate licence after that. Mrima Hill sits in a protected forest that members of the local Digo community treat as sacred, and residents have raised farms, burial grounds and ancestral sites in consultations so far. Consent on this hill is a real gate.

The one number

Kenyan reporting puts the deposit above 105 million tonnes, with in-ground value estimates between KSh 5.4 trillion and KSh 8.18 trillion. That figure measures minerals in the ground. Frontier has disclosed no investment amount and no construction timetable, a gap Africa Briefing flagged on November 19. The listed door into this consortium is small. AREC traded as high as $1.01 on November 19 and closed at $0.89, for a market value near $94 million. American Resources told investors on November 18 that it now holds about 14.4 percent of ReElement Technologies after recent spin-offs. An AREC shareholder owns a minority claim on one member of a four-member consortium that holds a notice of intention. Price it as exactly that.

Why Washington showed up

Two Chinese bidders, Hunan Research Institute for Nonferrous Metals and Jinan Yuxiao Group, withdrew from the tender in October. The US Department of State’s Bureau of African Affairs congratulated Frontier on X on November 19. Africa Briefing, citing the International Energy Agency, put China at about 85 percent of global rare earth refining in 2025. Ore in Kwale County still needs separation capacity, and ReElement’s refining technology is the strategic piece of this consortium. Nairobi played both sides on the same day. Joho’s announcement also named a Fangda Special Steel Technology and Sinohydro Corporation joint venture as the winner for chromite at Kang’ura and copper at Kamacabi. My position: Kenya ran an auction and sold access to both camps. Washington won the headline asset, and neither side owns a producing mine in Kenya today.

Watch three markers, in order. RareX, which bid with Iluka Resources and ranked second of three shortlisted groups, wants a formal debriefing by November 26 and says it will file a procurement complaint before the December 10 deadline, arguing Frontier missed the expression of interest deadline and was ineligible. Mrima Earth Limited, a group involving Critical Metals Corp and European Lithium, has also said it will challenge the award. Then watch for a prospecting licence filing, and for any investment figure from Frontier with a shilling amount attached. Until a licence prints, Mrima Hill is an option on a hill, and options lose value every month a process stalls.
